# Brand Research

Use this skill to turn an ambiguous brand brief into evidence a strategist and designer can act on. Work from customer interviews and internal materials first; use current competitor sites, product documentation, regulatory or trademark sources, and public customer language to test the context.

## Deliverable

Produce a compact research brief with audience jobs and buying barriers, category conventions worth using or avoiding, an observable competitor map, defensible proof points, and open questions such as naming, trademark, domain, or claim-risk checks.

## Working method

1. Separate facts, user claims, and hypotheses. Do not turn an unvalidated assumption into a brand promise.
2. Find the buying context: who approves, what makes the choice risky, and what outcome the buyer wants.
3. Audit competitors by message, proof, visual tone, interaction friction, and white space in the category. Avoid copying visual motifs simply because they recur.
4. Translate findings into opportunities: a useful tension, a differentiated position, and 3–5 creative territories. Each territory needs a product truth behind it.
5. State what must be validated before launch. Trademark availability and domain availability are separate checks.

Research must lead to decisions. Prefer a few sharp observations over generic trend lists. Cite the source of material claims and date-sensitive findings.
