# Brand Strategy

Use this skill to decide what a brand should mean before deciding what it should look like. It is for positioning, launch narratives, brand architecture, and message systems; it is not a substitute for trademark or legal review.

## Build a usable platform

Start with the commercial reality: the customer, their high-stakes job, the alternative they use today, and the proof the business can actually provide. Then articulate category and point of view; primary audience; positioning; promise and reasons to believe; personality defined by behaviors and boundaries; and 3–4 message pillars with evidence.

## Decision rules

- Differentiate with a specific truth: a process, expertise, source of proof, experience, or perspective. “AI-powered,” “innovative,” and “premium” are not differentiators without substance.
- Keep the promise proportionate to the actual product. For emerging products, frame the near-term offer accurately.
- A good proposition is legible in one sentence and expandable into a landing-page headline, sales narrative, and product behavior.
- Resolve tension instead of averaging it away: credible vs. energetic, expert vs. accessible, efficient vs. human. State which side leads and why.

Return a one-page brand platform followed by a creative brief for visual design. Name non-negotiables, open areas, and claims needing validation.
