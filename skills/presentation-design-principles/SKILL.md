# Presentation Design Principles

Use this skill when a deck must explain a brand, product, or strategic decision with clarity and a premium editorial feel. It applies to presentations and brand boards, not to dense reports or documentation that should be read at leisure.

## Design for a live room

Each slide must make one clear point. Move supporting explanation to a subsequent slide instead of compressing it into the first one. The presenter supplies the detail; the slide supplies the cue, evidence, or visual object that makes the point memorable.

For a sophisticated presentation, make restraint visible: use generous empty space, one dominant hierarchy, a limited number of elements, and purposeful contrasts. “Minimal” never means under-explained; it means each element has a job.

## Composition rules

- Establish a reading order with scale, contrast, position, and empty space before adding decorative elements.
- Group related material with proximity and shared alignment. Separate different ideas with real space, not only rules or boxes.
- Use a consistent spacing scale and align to an underlying grid. Do not introduce arbitrary gaps to repair individual slides.
- Reserve visual emphasis for the idea, decision, or asset that the audience should retain. Do not make every headline, word, or accent color compete.
- Let type remain readable at presentation distance. Test contrast for normal text at 4.5:1 and large text at 3:1 or better.
- Show the actual artifact whenever possible: an enlarged logo, a swatch, a motion frame, or a UI screen is better evidence than a paragraph describing it.

## Narrative sequence for a brand deck

1. Start with the commercial tension and the brand idea.
2. Explain the visual metaphor before revealing the finished mark.
3. Reveal one meaningful decision at a time: wordmark, symbol, color, typography, then system.
4. Move from foundations into evidence of use: product UI, mobile, social, icon, or environmental applications.
5. Close with the brand as a coherent operating system, not a gallery of isolated assets.

## Quality gate

Before delivery, evaluate the deck from a viewer’s perspective:

- Can someone state the slide’s main message after a three-second scan?
- Does the sequence create curiosity and then answer it in a logical order?
- Is the design sufficiently calm for a high-value brand, without becoming empty or repetitive?
- Do color, spacing, type, and imagery follow the same system?
- Are visual claims grounded in the strategy and product truth?
